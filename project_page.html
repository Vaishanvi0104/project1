1. Descriptive Analysis

1. The given dataset contains approximately one million records of transactions conducted in 2016.

2. It includes information such as transaction ID, customer ID, date of birth, location, and balance for each transaction.

3. The average account balance for customers during 2016 was 114,998 INR.

4. The average transaction amount was 1,566 INR.

5. Most account balances ranged between 4,728 and 57,428 INR.

6. The highest account balance recorded in 2016 was 11,035,495 INR.

7. The highest transaction conducted in 2016 amounted to 1,560,034 INR.

8. Mumbai had the highest number of transactions.

9. The highest number of transactions was recorded on August 7, 2016.

10. In terms of the number of transactions, the top three cities were Mumbai, New Delhi, and Bangalore, respectively.

11. Pune had 25,690 transactions in 2016.

12. The highest transaction amount from Pune was 1,380,002.88 INR.

13. Most citizens of Pune had account balances ranging from 4,957 to 56,108 INR.

14. Most transactions from Pune were in the range of 180 INR to 1,125 INR.

15. The number of transactions by males exceeded the number of transactions by females.

3. Statistical Analysis

Is there any significant correlation between account balance and transaction amount ?

  • In the correlation analysis between account balance and transaction amount, a positive correlation coefficient of 0.0627 was calculated.
  • The correlation is positive, but a value of 0.06 represents a very low correlation.
  • As a result, we can conclude that there exists a very weak relationship between account balance and transaction amount.

Is there significant difference in account balances between different locations ?

  • To test the hypothesis, an analysis of variance (ANOVA) test was conducted using the provided dataset. The dataset was grouped by location, and account balances within each location were collected into lists.
  • The F-statistic and p-value obtained from the ANOVA test were used to evaluate the differences in account balances across various locations.
  • The result of the ANOVA test indicates that there is no significant difference in account balances between different locations.
  • The p-value is greater than 0.05, which means that we fail to reject the null hypothesis.
  • This suggests that location does not have a significant impact on account balances.

Do gender have difference between average transaction amount ?

  • A t-test was conducted to assess the average transaction amounts between males and females.
  • The results strongly indicate that, on average, males have significantly higher transaction amounts compared to females.
  • This discrepancy may imply a potential gender-related gap in financial literacy and economic empowerment.
  • It suggests that males tend to be financially stronger than females in this context.

Customer Segmentation

Distribution of Customers :

  • Economical, Standard, and Premium are the three main customer groups based on account balances.
  • Economical customers make up the majority, constituting 99.21% of the customer base.
  • Standard customers account for 0.28%, while Premium customers represent 0.01% of the total clientele.
  • Gender distribution varies within these groups.

Gender Demographics :

  • Within the "Economical" group, 73.04% of customers are male, while 26.96% are female.
  • In the "Premium" group, the majority, 78.62%, are male customers, with 21.38% being female customers.
  • Among the "Standard" group, 79.49% are male customers, and 20.51% are female customers.

Time Based Analysis

Quarter wise Analysis :

  • Year-quarter wise transaction amounts were analyzed.
  • Most quarters showed consistent and stable transaction amounts.
  • Quarter 3 was exceptional, with transaction amounts approximately five times higher than others.
  • Consideration of seasonal patterns and external factors is warranted.
  • Findings can inform marketing and business strategies.
  • Future analysis may explore demographics, product usage, and economic indicators for deeper insights.

Hour wise Analysis :

  • Transaction Count by Hour was analyzed.
  • Hours with transaction counts exceeding 50,000 are considered "Busy Hours," including Hours 3, 6, 8, 9, 11, 14, and 16.
  • Hours with transaction counts below 30,000 are labeled as "Relax Hours," including Hours 20, 23, and 24.
  • Operational insights include system robustness during busy hours and efficient scheduling during relax hours.
  • Customer engagement strategies can be optimized based on busy and relax hour timing.
  • We can consider additional factors like day of the week for more refined analysis.
